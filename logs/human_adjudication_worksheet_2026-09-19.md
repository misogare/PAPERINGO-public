**What this measures.** The pipeline's verification verdict (admit or reject, as the manuscript uses it) is compared with an independent human reader's verdict on the same record. Scoring treats the reader as the reference: accuracy is the share of records on which the pipeline's verdict matches the reader's, with a 95% Wilson interval; sensitivity is the share of reader-admitted records the pipeline admitted; specificity the share of reader-rejected records it rejected; Cohen's kappa is reported with a bootstrap interval. Rescope verdicts are scored both ways (as admit and as reject).

**Instructions.** Work from the record's own claim and the cited studies only. For each item open the cited summary blocks in summaries.md (grep `^## Paper N:`) and the PDF listed in the `pdfs` column (data/paper_pdf_map.json; if the column is empty, find the PDF under downloads/). Do not consult the ledgers' status, note or review fields, the manuscript, or any earlier verdict for the record. Record one verdict per item in the CSV column `reader_verdict`: `admit` (the claim is supported by the cited studies as stated), `rescope` (supported only with a narrower scope; say what in `reader_note`), or `reject` (not supported, or the cited studies do not show it). One line of reasoning per item in `reader_note`; note the page or block field the decision rests on. Budget about 10 to 15 minutes per item. Then run `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 python scripts/score_double_adjudication.py --date 2026-09-19 --prefix human_adjudication`, which reports accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and kappa with intervals, per-kind accuracy, and lists every disagreement.
